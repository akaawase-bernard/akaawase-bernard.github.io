I Built My Own Private Cloud for the Price of a Hard Drive

October 9, 2026

For years I paid, or put up with limited free storage, to keep my files, photos, and research on someone else's servers. Then I realized I had a Raspberry Pi sitting on my desk. With a 2TB drive plugged in, it now works as my own private cloud, which I can reach from anywhere.

The setup is simple. I used a Raspberry Pi 4B, a computer the size of a deck of cards that sips electricity, and a 2TB external drive for storage. On the software side, I run Nextcloud, free open-source software that works like Google Drive or Dropbox, and Tailscale, a free tool that lets my devices reach the Pi securely from anywhere. The Pi and the drive were the only hardware I needed, and everything else was free software.

The Pi sits at home, connected to my router, running Nextcloud, and the drive holds the files. Tailscale creates a private, encrypted tunnel between the Pi and my phone and laptop, so I can reach my files from a café or on mobile data without exposing the Pi to the open internet. That matters because a home server that is directly reachable online is a target. With Tailscale, only my own devices can see it.

I use it every day. My laptop syncs research and document folders to the Pi, just like Dropbox, so my work is always in sync. My iPhone uploads new photos automatically, so I'm no longer paying for photo storage. Family members each get their own account, so photos and files stay private.

I made a few mistakes along the way. My first attempt had the drive mounting in the wrong place, and files would have quietly filled the Pi's small memory card instead of the 2TB drive, so I now always check where storage is actually mounted before saving anything. The first file-sharing app I tried also turned out to be shutting down, with no more security fixes coming. That's not something you want guarding your files, so I switched to Nextcloud, which is actively maintained. And I would not skip the boring security steps: use strong passwords, keep software updated, and never open your router to the internet just for convenience.

Is it really free? Not quite, but nearly. You pay a few dollars a year in electricity and need a home internet connection. Your home upload speed also sets how fast files come back to you when you're away, so a slow connection will feel slow.

The most important thing to remember is that syncing is not backup. If you delete a file on one device, it can vanish everywhere, and if the drive fails, everything on it is gone. A private cloud is convenient, but you should keep a second copy of anything important on another drive or a different service. I treat the Pi as my main storage and keep a separate backup for the work I can't afford to lose.

Should you try it? If you're comfortable following step-by-step instructions and typing a few commands, yes. It took me an afternoon, most of it spent fixing small mistakes, and I came out with storage I control, no monthly fee, and a better understanding of how the cloud actually works. If you have a spare Raspberry Pi, this is a great weekend project.
